Why this Library?

Self development/education is my purpose and reason for this library, but its not enough just to say so. We suffer many modern social crisis', and we know education is close to the root of it, both as cause and potential solution. A hundred years of reforms, however, has only deepened the crisis and decline. One cannot fix what is misunderstood. I offer no magic formula. You are the last stand for your own development, if desired at all, or is to happen at all, but it must come through self discovery.

"Know thyself"

This is a maxim which was inscribed upon the Temple of Apollo at Delphi, 2,500 years ago. Plato understood it to mean, broadly speaking, "know your soul". I would add, "discover how to be more human." Becoming more human is the real evolution of mankind.

"The mind is not a vessel to be filled but a fire to be kindled." - Plutach.

"What education is to the individual, revelation is to the whole human race." - Gotthold Ephraim Lessing

"The unexamined life is not worth living." - Socrates

Taken seriously, how many lives are unexamined? These words are the seeds of individual fire, and would completely transform one's life forever. By catching fire only a littel, Modernity would pass into history.

"All men by nature desire knowledge" - Aristotle

We are not here for entertainment or endless speculations but to accomplish our reason for living. There is always a tension between the Moderns and the Ancients when looking at who we are and how we should live. One must be clear which impulse we are under and which is worth following consiously.

Where We Stand Today

"The problem in our country isn't with books being banned, but with people no longer reading. You don't have to burn books to destroy a culture. Just get people to stop reading them." —Ray Bradbury

I owe Project Gutenberg an unpayable debt. But wade into that catalog and you'll drown in mediocrity — thousands of forgettable books preserved with the same care as the handful of genuinely great ones. Meanwhile, real literature, the stuff that would wreck you if you read it, sits buried two clicks deeper, untouched, because nobody thought to dig it out, or nobody's reading what's already sitting in plain sight. I'm doing my small part digging. But it's one man against a landfill.

That's a glimpse of the problem we must solve. I have always dreamed of collecting the best books in the world, first for myself, and for people who educate themselves and others, and this growing collection is my pushback against the landfill. While I cannot claim to have found all the best books, yet, (smile), you can judge for yourself if you are improved by what you find here.

What you'll find is a collection of what I think are the best and most interesting books, always growing and refining. It's intended to be a real resource for the curious, the truth seeker, the learner, the educator, and the self-educator.

Public Libraries are always a mixed bag of good and bad, because they try to be all things to all people. Books form an important foundation for education however, so it matters to distinguish a good book from a bad one — contrary as that is to the modern mindset. This is my chance to put forward my best "books."

To rise above the landfill, I focus on what I find excites the mind and the self discovery of higher human self, rather than duplicating programs or other popular libraries.

What Books You Will Find Here.

The strategy is simple: search, find, read and share the best books. There is no complicated criteria, except the question, is this a great book? or does this book look interesting? Many books have fallen off to make room for better books. I have found so many books, especially in the Public Domain, which deserve a good look. This website proves that a self directed education, (the only real kind of education) is really not expensive, except for your time and dedication and clearness of purpose.

People ask if I have read all these books. No, but when I find great books, I get a clearer picture of what they are and who is writing them.

Some examples may serve here. Consider Isaac Asimov, a remarkable writer. Most people don't know he wrote a brilliant historical series: Historia Universal Asimov Series. Ive read The Greeks, The Roman Republic and The Roman Empire, all favorites. I list the rest of the series too.

I found Will Durant to have some of the best popular historical prose writing to be found (opposed to dry academic writing). I list his eleven volume set, The Story of Civilization.

The Classics are always a breath of fresh air when reading directly and not about them. There are many adapted Classics in public domain very suitable for young readers as well. Many such books are prized like those of authors like Alfred Church, Jacob Abbott and his brother John Abbott.

The spiritual works must also be not be left out. The Bible, Buddhist (The Life of the Buddha) and Indian scriptures (The Bhagavad Gita and the Mahabharata) are the greatest treasures to be experienced. Nor the subjects that fall outside modern boundaries are to be ignored. Consider Plato and his discussion of ancient Atlantis. Those who refer to them as "Myth," certainly did not get that from Plato. So this example, drawn from the classics themselves, normally out of sight, is a source of great interest.

The discovery of Rudolph Steiner is for me the capstone of knowledge. I don't say that lightly. When the normal paths of learning can no longer satisfy, one is compelled to reach further. Steiner's many hundreds of works are well represented here.

The Problem is Much Worse/Better Than We Think.

The conscious and intelligent manipulation of the organized habits and opinions of the masses is an important element in democratic society. Those who manipulate this unseen mechanism of society constitute an invisible government which is the true ruling power of our country.

We are governed, our minds are molded, our tastes formed, our ideas suggested, largely by men we have never heard of. This is a logical result of the way our democratic society is organized. -PROPAGANDA By EDWARD L. BERNAYS, 1928

100 years later, we are way past the warning stage. Those who have heard nor heeded the warning are too asleep to respond and remain vulnerable to the modern ills. The destructive effects of propaganda on an unsuspecting society and individuals are deep and wide and personal. Consider the already advanced stage of mass illiteracy, mass depression, mass medication, mass illness. The last decade has exposed our fictional view of the world, how it works, and who we are. but there is still much work exposing to be done.

We live our lives in a blind channel. Our youthful powers are deadened at school and work. Imagine the power of youth at speaking, reading, writing, new languages, music, dance, etc., all lost potential because parents themselves have lost the impulse of self education for generations. this is the opposite of evolution and progress.

Reading good books is certainly a necessary habit to regain some upward impulse. The Liberal arts have not lost their usefulness, (see the Trivium and Dororothy Sayers, the Lost Tools of Learning).

The good news is I believe a sea change is happening in our time. Where it leads has yet to be seen, but I can wish for the best, and I certainly do.

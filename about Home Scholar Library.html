Disclaimer: I'm really an enthusiast and autodidact and not a scholar per se.

I owe Project Gutenberg an unpayable debt. But wade into that catalog and you'll drown in mediocrity — thousands of forgettable books preserved with the same care as the handful of genuinely great ones. Meanwhile, real literature, the stuff that would wreck you if you read it, sits buried two clicks deeper, untouched, because nobody thought to dig it out, or nobody's reading what's already sitting in plain sight. I'm doing my small part digging. But it's one man against a landfill.

That's a glimpse of the problem we must solve. I have always dreamed of collecting the best books in the world, first for myself, and for people who educate themselves and others, and this growing collection is my pushback against the landfill. While I cannot claim to have found all the best books yet, (smile), you can judge for yourself if you are improved by what you find here.

What you'll find is a collection of what I think are the best and most interesting books, always growing and refining. It's intended to be a real resource for the curious, the truth seeker, the learner, the educator, and the self-educator.

Libraries are always a mixed bag of good and bad, because they try to be all things to all people. Books are the foundation of education however, so it matters to distinguish a good book from a bad one — contrary as that is to the modern mindset. This is my chance to put forward my best "books."

To rise above the landfill, I focus on what's genuinely hard to find rather than duplicating what's already everywhere. I avoid great books that are easily available online — Gibbon, Dickens, Dumas, Shakespeare, Jane Austen — all great, and already very popular.

What I focus on instead:

  1. Alternate translations of classics by Homer, Herodotus, Ovid, Livy, Virgil, Dante — translations not yet widely available or hard to find. For example, I wouldn't post the legendary Alexander Pope's Iliad or Samuel Butler's prose translation, so as not to duplicate books that are already easy to find.

  2. Interesting public-domain discoveries useful for young readers and homeschoolers — great historical biographies, histories, and the like.

  3. Abridgments. I've found real abridgment gems of great authors like Gibbon, Hume, Guizot, Michelet, Symonds, Froissart, Michaud, and others.

  4. Spiritual works, philosophical works, dramatic literature.

  5. Related media — audiobooks, film, YouTube productions, and art.
